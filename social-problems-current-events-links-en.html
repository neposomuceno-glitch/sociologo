Social Problems: Current-Events and Media Links

A companion subpage to the sociology resources list — sites that track social problems as they happen, in real time (as opposed to academic theory or associations). Organized by type. Language and paywall status noted where relevant.

1. Global monitoring bodies (official / intergovernmental)

  • UN World Social Report — the UN DESA's flagship annual report on global social trends (inequality, insecurity, trust). Open access.
  • World Bank – Poverty — global poverty data, live indicators, blog. Open access.
  • World Inequality Database (WID) — open-access global data on income/wealth inequality, interactive maps. Academic consortium, free.
  • UNHCR — real-time coverage of the global refugee and displacement crisis.
  • OCHA ReliefWeb — UN humanitarian crisis updates, disaster and conflict tracking, global.

2. Inequality and poverty (advocacy + data)

  • Inequality.org — a US-based but globally focused portal on income/wealth inequality, weekly newsletter. Open access.
  • Oxfam – Inequality & Poverty — advocacy reports (e.g. the annual Davos inequality report). Open access.

3. Human rights

  • Amnesty International — global human rights reports, campaigns, country-by-country updates. Open access, multiple languages.
  • Human Rights Watch — investigative reports on human rights worldwide. Open access.

4. Investigative journalism on social issues

  • ProPublica — a nonprofit investigative outlet focused on the US but widely cited; strong topic pages on immigration, criminal justice, health, and labor. Open access.
  • Devex — global journalism on development and humanitarian aid (aid cuts, refugee crises, NGOs). Mostly open, some premium content.
  • The Conversation — academics writing accessibly about current social issues; has regional editions (UK, US, Africa, Latin America/Spain via "The Conversation" network). Open access.

5. Regional / non-English-language coverage

  • Jadaliyya — a publication of the Arab Studies Institute on Middle Eastern political/social issues. Arabic and English, open access.
  • Platform for Investigative Journalism (Malawi) — an example of a regional investigative outlet covering local social crises (human trafficking, refugee camps) often overlooked by global media.
  • Nation Online (Malawi), Club of Mozambique, and similar national outlets — useful for ground-level coverage of specific crises; search by country name + "news" to find local equivalents elsewhere.

6. Topic-specific trackers

  • Migration/refugees: UNHCR (above), IOM – International Organization for Migration
  • Housing crisis: World Bank housing data, UN-Habitat
  • Gender inequality: UN Women
  • Mental health: WHO Mental Health

Usage notes (this subpage)

  • Several of these (ProPublica, The Conversation, Devex) are journalism, not peer-reviewed research — useful for tracking what's happening now, not for citing as academic sources.
  • Country-specific crises are usually best followed through local rather than global media, which tend to cover only the most visible cases (e.g., the funding crisis at a Malawian refugee camp surfaced mainly through Devex and a small Malawian investigative outlet, not the major global papers).
  • For non-English-speaking regions not covered here (East Asia, Francophone Africa, South Asia beyond India), a dedicated section can be built on request.

Caveats carried over from the earlier research on sociology sites

These apply to the broader sociology link lists this subpage complements: - Link stability varies by type. Association and journal websites tend to be stable long-term. Blog URLs — especially The Society Pages' community blogs (Cyborgology, Sociological Images, etc.) — change or go dormant more easily; worth verifying they're still active before citing formally. - Language coverage is uneven. Many national sociology associations outside the US/UK/Western Europe (Germany's DGS, China's CSSN, Japan's sociology bodies) publish mainly in their own language, with sparse or nonexistent English sections — useful for regional context but a barrier for English-only readers. - Coverage gaps persist for continental Africa and the Arabic-speaking sociological world beyond Jadaliyya, and no live, clearly identifiable site for a Russian national sociology association was found. If any of these regions is a priority, it would need additional, more targeted searching. - General note on sources: across all these lists, official/intergovernmental sites (UN, World Bank) and academic association sites are the most durable references; advocacy sites (Oxfam, Inequality.org) carry a point of view worth flagging when cited; journalism sites are timely but not peer-reviewed.
