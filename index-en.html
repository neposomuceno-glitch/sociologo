The Study of Society as a Global Endeavor

The homepage of the whole project: sociology as it is practiced, organized, and debated worldwide — its institutions, its ongoing crises, its people. Keep all files in the same folder so the links below work.

Where to find sociology, worldwide

Sociology Websites Directory The master directory of the discipline itself: international bodies (ISA, CODESRIA, CLACSO, ALAS), national and regional associations on every continent — including country-by-country sociologists' colleges and associations across Latin America (Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Mexico, Peru, and more) —, dozens of subfields and their specialized societies (from criminology to digital sociology and world-systems theory), media and communications research, community-based participatory research outside academia, open-access journals, and public sociology spaces. Includes two live, debated policy contrasts — policing versus community-based violence prevention, and school-based prevention versus medication — presented from multiple perspectives rather than resolved. Every source is tagged 🔓 open / 🪪 registration required / 🔒 paywalled.

Where social problems are happening now

Social Problems: Current-Events and Media Links Sites that track social problems in real time, not theory: global monitoring bodies (UN, World Bank), inequality and poverty portals, human rights organizations, investigative journalism, and regional coverage beyond the English-language press.

Social Instability Maps: Link Directory Links to existing, live, expert-maintained interactive maps — the Fragile States Index, the ACLED conflict tracker, FEWS NET's famine early-warning maps, the Global Peace Index, the Global Organized Crime Index — rather than static snapshots, since these update on their own schedule and are the actual primary sources.

Social Instability Heat Map (interactive — open in a browser) A single composite severity map built from those cited indices, color-coded red/orange/yellow, with a source given for each rating.

Social Instability Maps by Type (interactive — open in a browser) The expanded version: twelve separate tabbed maps — armed conflict, famine, economic inequality, organized crime, disease outbreaks, corruption, drug production and trafficking, violence against women, violence against minorities, press freedom, economic migration, and religious freedom/persecution — each with its own metric, its own link to a live dashboard with the primary source, and a full reference list with open-access status. Includes a short essay on why mapping the social world reveals things a table of numbers can't: spatial clustering, contagion across borders, and the geography behind why instability concentrates where it does.

Who built the field of understanding

Key Contributors to Understanding Social Problems Around twenty-five figures — all trained sociologists, classic and contemporary, Western and non-Western — whose work diagnosed a social problem and proposed a concrete response: from Ibn Khaldun's theory of social cohesion in the 14th century, through Jane Addams's Hull House and Du Bois's empirical sociology of race, to contemporary work on eviction, mass incarceration, medicalization, world-systems theory, social capital, and the network society. Each entry links to a fuller biography and, where one exists, a portrait or drawing of the person.

Sociological Theorists and Philosophers of Social Problems The companion to the page above: thirty-four large-scale theorists — from Confucius, Plato, and Aristotle in antiquity, through Hobbes, Rousseau, and Comte in the Enlightenment, to Foucault, Habermas, and Rawls in the 20th century — whose work builds general frameworks for understanding society, power, or justice, rather than diagnosing and solving a specific social problem.

Selected Works, by Contributor A matching bibliography for every name on the contributors page — each person's principal books and essays, linked so you can find an edition, translation, or library copy.

How sociology has been used against the societies it studies

Sociology in the Service of Sponsors: Social Control, Diplomacy, and Hybrid Warfare An uncomfortable but well-documented chapter: Project Camelot, the Human Terrain System, Cold War cultural diplomacy, population control as a geopolitical instrument, philanthropic shaping of the research agenda, and contemporary hybrid/information warfare — alongside the ethical and critical response the discipline itself generated toward each episode.

How the whole project fits together

  • The directories point outward, to the living institutions, journals, and organizations that make up the discipline.
  • The interactive maps are self-contained analyses built from the sources cited in those directories.
  • The contributors and theorists pages are about the people whose ideas feed the whole discipline — and this project — with cross-references to the directories where their organizations and journals actually live.
